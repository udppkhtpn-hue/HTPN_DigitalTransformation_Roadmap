# Built From Within — 20-minute keynote script

Nov 1, 2026 · @Fairis

## How to use this script

The script runs 20 minutes: 2,757 spoken words at 140 words a minute (19:41), plus about 30 seconds where the Innovathon reel plays on its own. It covers all 20 sheets and 49 presses, and every figure in it comes from the deck brief.

Each sheet shows its spoken time and word count. The line in square brackets is what the press brings up, so click at the start of the paragraph beneath it. The last line of each sheet leads into the next click.

The talk has four acts plus a close. Acts 1 and 2 are the proof: they take about seven minutes, so keep them brisk. Act 3 is the turn: slow down there, because that's where the room decides whether to trust you. Act 4 is the roadmap, the reason you're on stage, and gets half the time.

Three delivery notes:

- **Let the screen go first.** On sheets with a big reveal (S-01, S-05, S-06, S-08), wait until the animation lands before you speak. The room can't read and listen at the same time.
- **Look at the judges once.** On S-08, when you thank the Innovathon judges, look at them. It's the one personal moment in the talk.
- **Leave a pause before the last two lines.** On S-20, after "share it with every hospital in KKM", stop for two seconds, then say "Built from within. Shared with all of KKM." slowly.

## Act 1 · Why and how we started (S-01 to S-04, about 3:23)

### S-01 Cold open  (45s, 105 words)

*\[Press 1: three lines land, the 1889 building draws in, then the title. Start speaking when the title appears.\]*

Good afternoon. My name is Fairis. I'm the Acting Deputy Director of Hospital Tengku Permaisuri Norashikin in Kajang. It's a public hospital, part of the Ministry of Health, and it is a hundred and thirty-five years old. The drawing behind me is where it all started.

Eighteen months ago, a small team of clinicians in that hospital made a decision. We would not wait for a vendor. We would build our own digital tools, ourselves, from within.

In the next twenty minutes I'll show you what that produced, what it exposed, and where we take it next. It starts with a date.

### S-02 Ahead of the mandate  (38s, 88 words)

*\[Press 1: The 5 June 2026 Cabinet directive, with the newspaper cutting\]*

On the fifth of June this year, the Cabinet directed that all government digital services be developed in-house. Three reasons were given: data sovereignty, government access to source code, and building talent inside the civil service.

*\[Press 2: The timeline draws back to April 2025 and measures 14 months\]*

We started in April 2025. Fourteen months before the directive. And those three reasons were already exactly why we were doing it. We weren't reacting to a policy. By the time it arrived, we were already a working example of it. So why did we start so early? Because we had no choice.

### S-03 Three forces  (69s, 163 words)

*\[Press 1: Pressure, +128% in red\]*

Three forces pushed us. The first is pressure. Our beds are full and our staff are tired. Then this June, dengue arrived. Admissions were up a hundred and twenty-eight percent on the year before. That kind of surge bends any system. And births have almost tripled since we moved from the old building into the new Women and Child Centre, from two hundred to more than five hundred a month.

*\[Press 2: Vendor lock-in\]*

The second is vendor lock-in. In a public hospital, a new system means procurement. Procurement is slow, and every change request after that has a price. And when the system belongs to someone else, our data can leave our control.

*\[Press 3: Opportunity, then the footer line\]*

The third is opportunity. For the first time, AI and low-code tools let a clinician build a working tool in weeks. So the limit was no longer technology. It was leadership. Change fails when it is delegated. It succeeds when it is led. So we decided to lead it. Here is how.

### S-04 How we led it  (61s, 142 words)

*\[Press 1: Four boxes are pencilled in with Kotter's eight steps named; stage 1, "Leaders learn first", fills in and the 2025 photographs start to drift underneath.\]*

We ran this as a change programme, not an IT project. Underneath it sits Kotter's eight steps. In practice, it came down to four moves.

First, leaders learn first. Before asking anyone to change, we went back to school: Monash, this programme with MIT Sloan and ASB, and LSE. Credibility before mandate.

*\[Press 2: Stage 2, "Our own syllabus".\]*

Second, our own syllabus, with one rule. Everyone leaves having built something. Not watched a demo. Built.

*\[Press 3: Stage 3, "Quick wins".\]*

Third, quick wins. Small scope, shipped in weeks, visible to the people on the wards.

*\[Press 4: Stage 4, "Reward the builders".\]*

Fourth, reward the builders. That became a national Innovathon.

For a picture of insourcing at scale, we looked to a bank.

*\[Press 5: The photographs give way; the split-flap turns 85% outsourced into 85% insourced.\]*

DBS Bank moved from eighty-five percent outsourced to eighty-five percent insourced. A bank, not a hospital. But the lesson travels. The capability you build inside is the capability you keep. Here is what ours looks like today.

## Act 2 · What we built (S-05 to S-08, about 3:24)

### S-05 Results  (34s, 79 words)

*\[Press 1: All four figures count up, then the footnote. Let them land before you speak.\]*

More than thirteen hundred healthcare staff trained. More than fifty in-house apps in active use. Thirty-five workshops, and fifteen of those happened because someone outside our hospital asked for one. And the number I'm proudest of: zero ringgit in software licensing.

These are October figures. And every one of those apps was built by people who already had a full clinical or management job. Those fifteen invitations told us something, so let me show you where they came from.

### S-06 Where we've taught  (55s, 128 words)

*\[Press 1: The map draws; arcs fly from Kajang to 15 sites in date order; the counter ticks\]*

We never pushed this model on anyone. Every one of these was an invitation. Watch the counter.

Fifteen sessions in twelve months. Hospitals. Community groups. And whole national services: the northern, central and southern O&G services, Family Medicine, Medical Services. When a national service invites you, you teach a whole discipline at once.

From Alor Setar in the north to Johor Bahru in the south, and across the sea to Kuching. And it is speeding up. Five of the fifteen came in the last two months.

*\[Press 2: Cyan rings pulse on sites running tools built on the model\]*

Then came the part that matters most. The hospitals we taught started building their own tools. Marudi. Sri Aman. Mukah. Penang. Johor. That is the moment a training programme becomes a movement. We taught. They built.

Here is what has been built.

### S-07 The portfolio  (39s, 91 words)

*\[Press 1: Three scrolling lanes of app names and the showcase QR code\]*

These are real tools in daily use, sorted by whose day they improve. For patients: GynaeClerk, AntenatalClerk, a fetal kick chart. For doctors: theatre scheduling, an AI blood-loss estimator, a clinical document checker. For management: an emergency department dashboard, rostering, governance audits.

And look closely. Some of these weren't built by us at all. They came from the hospitals we taught.

Built by clinicians, for clinicians. The QR code takes you to every one of them. Scan it now, or after the session.

So we brought these builders into one room.

### S-08 Innovathon  (46s + about 30s of reel, 108 words)

*\[Press 1: The reel plays. Say one line, then stay silent for about 30 seconds.\]*

In July, we took it national.

*\[Press 2: About 30 seconds in, click. The reel keeps running behind; the counters build.\]*

The AI Productivity Intelligence Innovathon, on the twenty-first of July. Two hundred delegates. Twenty-eight finalists. Nine winners. Thirteen external judges from industry, academia and government. Some of you sat on that panel, and I want to thank you for it. BERNAMA covered the day.

Several of the winners came from hospitals that had adopted our model. And the model is spreading: Hospital Pulau Pinang, Kepala Batas, Sultanah Aminah, JKN Sarawak, and the national O&G, FMS and Internal Medicine services.

So by July we had a culture, a portfolio and a national network. That is the good news. Now for the honest part.

## Act 3 · The gap (S-09 to S-10, about 2:13)

Slow down here. This is where the room decides whether to trust you.

### S-09 The gap  (66s, 155 words)

*\[Press 1: Fifty drifting dots under "50 apps ≠ one hospital"\]*

Success created a new problem. It is one everyone in this room will recognise. Fifty apps is not one hospital. Each dot is a tool that works on its own.

*\[Press 2: The dots dim; the first gap, "Fragmentation", appears\]*

There are four gaps. Fragmentation: separate spreadsheets that rarely talk to each other or to the hospital information system.

*\[Press 3: The second gap, "Sustainability"\]*

Sustainability: many tools depend on one builder. If that person moves on, the tool stalls.

*\[Press 4: The third gap, "Governance"\]*

Governance: registration, PDPA data classes and rules for AI have not caught up, which leaves us open to being called shadow IT.

*\[Press 5: The fourth gap, "Evidence", then the MIT CISR line\]*

And evidence: few tools have before-and-after measures, so we cannot yet say with confidence which ones to scale.

MIT CISR gives us a useful lens. We are strong in shared insights, and we have a growing developer community. We are weak in operational backbone and accountability. We have the community. We do not yet have the backbone.

And when you zoom out from Kajang, the gap gets much bigger.

### S-10 The national picture  (67s, 156 words)

*\[Press 1: 155 circles; the 20 with THIS fill in; counters 20 and 135\]*

The Ministry of Health runs a hundred and fifty-five hospitals. Each circle is one of them. Twenty have THIS, the Total Hospital Information System. A hundred and thirty-five are still manual.

*\[Press 2: The rest light up year by year until all 155 are lit; RM 1 billion appears\]*

THIS is coming. The plan adds sixteen hospitals this year, twenty-six next year, then forty-four, then forty-nine. By 2029, every circle is lit, on a budget of one billion ringgit. That is the right investment. And I want to be clear: we are not competing with it.

*\[Press 3: The circles dim; the work-process line, the three sites, the amber closing line\]*

But here is what we found on the ground. Even in hospitals that have THIS, it does not cover the whole work process. We saw that ourselves at Hospital Cyberjaya, at Hospital Port Dickson, and in our own Women and Child Centre in Kajang.

So our apps fill two gaps. The years of waiting before THIS arrives. And the work THIS was never designed to cover. That is why our roadmap cannot stop at Kajang. First, the destination.

## Act 4 · The roadmap (S-11 to S-19, about 9:59)

This is why you're on stage. Half the talk lives here.

### S-11 Vision 2028  (85s, 199 words)

*\[Press 1: The vision sentence writes itself in\]*

By 2028 we want two things. A public hospital that sees its patient flow in real time and owns its digital solutions. And a lighthouse: a hospital whose proven tools any KKM hospital can adopt. Not an island.

*\[Press 2: Four hospital outlines stand on a scale line, smallest to largest\]*

One ministry, very different hospitals. A Type 4 district hospital may have thirty to a hundred and fifty beds. Type 3 and Type 2 hospitals carry minor and major specialty disciplines. A state hospital has more than a thousand beds. You know this problem well: one product, very different markets. So not every tool should travel.

*\[Press 3: Three reach rings draw out from HTPN\]*

We sort our apps by how far they can travel. Some are facility-specific. They fit our setup, and they stay local. Some belong to a service or a discipline. Our O&G tools are already used by the national O&G service, so they travel to every hospital that runs that service. And some are general. A patient-journey app works in a thirty-bed district hospital and in a thousand-bed state hospital alike. Build locally. Scale what travels.

*\[Press 4: The five objectives slam in along the bottom\]*

That gives us five objectives. Govern what we build. Integrate for patient flow. Prove the value. Sustain the capability. And scale what travels. Here is the route, in five phases.

### S-12 Roadmap  (135s, 316 words)

*\[Press 1: Phase 1, Ignite, ACHIEVED; two rails, HTPN above and KKM below\]*

The roadmap runs on two rails. On top, HTPN as the lighthouse. Underneath, KKM, scaling what travels. Every phase has to move both.

Phase one, Ignite, is done. It is everything you have seen so far: the culture at home, and a model other hospitals and national services already use.

*\[Press 2: The pin drops on Phase 2; the workshop card and photos open\]*

Phase two, Protect and Showcase, has already begun. On the nineteenth of October, about twenty of our Innovathon builders came back to Kajang for a MyIPO and innovation workshop. Ts. Mohd Azirudin bin Sahhudin of Politeknik Port Dickson and Puan Norshariza binti Jamhuri of Hospital Kuala Lumpur taught us two things: how to protect our innovations as intellectual property, and how to prepare entries for Karnival Inovasi KKM. Protection first. Then national exposure.

*\[Press 3: Phase 3, Consolidate and Govern\]*

Phase three, Consolidate and Govern, runs from this month to June 2027. Every app goes into a registry, tiered by risk, with a build lifecycle, data governance and impact baselines. On the KKM rail, every app is tagged by reach: facility, service or general. It is done when every tool is registered, owned and tagged.

*\[Press 4: Phase 4, Integrate\]*

Phase four, Integrate, runs from July 2027 to June 2028. For HTPN, that is our flagship, a hospital Command Centre. For KKM, we package our general patient-journey apps as templates other hospitals can configure and deploy, and we offer service-specific apps through their national services. It is done when the Command Centre is used every day, patient flow beats baseline, and at least one general app runs at another KKM hospital.

*\[Press 5: Phase 5, Predict and Scale\]*

Phase five, Predict and Scale, starts in July 2028. At home, forecasting and governed clinical AI. Across KKM, a hub-and-spoke playbook for every hospital type, from the thirty-bed district hospital to the thousand-bed state hospital. It is done when other KKM sites adopt it.

Six enablers sit under all five, from people to funding and partnerships. Let me take you inside, starting with the least glamorous phase.

### S-13 Phase 3: the registry  (50s, 117 words)

*\[Press 1: The fifty dots return and sort into three tier bands\]*

Phase three turns those fifty scattered dots into a portfolio the hospital can rely on, in three tiers.

Experimental tools use dummy or de-identified data, and anyone can build them freely. Departmental tools are in routine use in one unit. They need a named owner, documentation and a backup maintainer. Hospital-critical tools cross departments or touch patient safety. They get full governance, a security review and impact measures.

*\[Press 2: The footer line in amber\]*

The principle is governance in proportion to risk. Experiments stay easy, so people keep building. Critical tools meet a full standard, so patients stay safe. Load every experiment with paperwork, and you kill the culture that got us here.

And underneath that registry sits work nobody will ever see.

### S-14 Phase 3: the foundations  (55s, 128 words)

*\[Press 1: Four piles drive down under the building, one at a time\]*

Four foundations, and everything rests on them.

A build lifecycle: version control, a clear line between demo and production, and handover notes, so a tool can outlive its builder.

Data governance: PDPA data classes, and an on-premises route for any AI work that touches sensitive data.

Registration: with RKB, with MyIPO, and a standing link with JP ICT, our IT colleagues. We build with them, not around them. That amber tick is the October workshop. We have already started.

And impact baselines, using our own twenty-week roster study as the template for how we measure.

*\[Press 2: The dimension line stamps "Done when"\]*

Phase three is done when every tool is registered, and every hospital-critical tool has an owner, a backup and a baseline. With those foundations in, we can build what this hospital actually needs.

### S-15 Command Centre  (64s, 150 words)

*\[Press 1: The fifty dots flow onto six streams that converge on the hub\]*

The HTPN Command Centre. One real-time view of patient flow.

Six streams we already own, flowing into one place. Beds, from Bed Watcher. The emergency department dashboard. Theatre scheduling. Transfer requests. Discharge and step-down, including our Short Stay Unit under Project BRIDGE. And the staffing roster.

Notice there is no new system to buy. Every stream comes from a tool we already built. Integration is what turns fifty apps into one hospital.

*\[Press 2: The hub turns red; the alert and the evidence lines appear\]*

And it raises its own alarm. Automated surge alerts, built on our own dengue Yellow and Orange thresholds.

The case for it comes from our own wards. Focused flow work at PDL and Ward 72 has already cut boarding time by thirteen point three percent. And during this year's dengue surge, every decision about beds ran on data gathered by hand. We do not want to meet the next surge that way.

Here is how we build it.

### S-16 Command Centre: how we build it  (57s, 134 words)

*\[Press 1: Jul 2027 to Jun 2028; two milestones stamped in order\]*

Small first. Over twelve months, from July 2027, the minimum viable view comes first: beds and emergency department flow. Then automated surge alerts, live before the 2027 to 2028 dengue season. Two milestones the morning bed meeting will actually feel.

*\[Press 2: Four governance rules appear as pencilled notes, then "Done when"\]*

And it is governed from day one. It reads from the tools we already have, with read-only feeds from the hospital information system where agreed. Managers see aggregate views, and every access to identifiable data is logged. And because spreadsheets will hit their limits, this phase includes an architecture review to choose an approved data layer.

It is done when it is used in the daily bed meeting, the alerts are automatic, and boarding time and emergency department stay beat baseline. Once we can see the present clearly, we can start to look ahead.

### S-17 Predict and scale  (44s, 102 words)

*\[Press 1: The headline widens from "seeing now" to "anticipating next", then the list\]*

From seeing now, to anticipating next. Phase five runs on what the Command Centre gives us: a year of clean, integrated data.

That lets us forecast admissions and bed demand. It lets us move clinical AI, document checking and ICD-11 coding, into production, always with a clinician signing off. It lets us take the innovation pipeline forward: facial PPG triage, specimen robots and drones, a virtual clinic for a prison. And it lets us package all of it as a hub-and-spoke playbook for KKM.

Ambition is easy to put on a slide. So here is how we will hold ourselves to it.

### S-18 Measuring success  (43s, 100 words)

*\[Press 1: The schedule table draws row by row; Phase 1 in amber; then the note\]*

Every phase has its indicators. Phase one is in amber because it has already happened. Phase four, for example, is measured in the bed meeting: boarding time, bed occupancy and emergency department stay against baseline.

You will notice there are no targets for boarding time or length of stay. That is deliberate. We will set them from our Phase three baselines, from our own data, rather than invent them for a slide.

We also track the health of the programme itself: active builders, tools with a backup maintainer, staff satisfaction. Because the biggest risk to this roadmap is not technical.

### S-19 Risks  (66s, 154 words)

*\[Press 1: The red stamp lands; the first register row appears in red\]*

The biggest risk is already here. It is key people. This whole effort runs on a handful of people doing it on top of their clinical and management work. So protected time or formal posts, departmental champions, and a backup maintainer for every tool are not extras. They are the plan.

*\[Press 2: The rest of the risk register draws in\]*

The rest will look familiar to anyone who has scaled anything. Being seen as shadow IT: we partner early with our IT unit and JP ICT. A data protection breach: tiered data classes, de-identified by default, audit logs and human review of AI. Resistance to integration: start with read-only feeds, and prove value with our own data first. And burnout: keep the Innovathon, keep the wins coming, keep the rules proportionate.

None of these is a reason to slow down. Each is a reason to make the next phase about structure. And that brings me to the lesson for me as a leader.

## Close (S-20, about 1:01)

### S-20 Lesson & close  (61s, 143 words)

*\[Press 1: "Phase 1 was won through culture."\]*

Phase one was won through culture. Energy, example, recognition. Leaders who learned first. Builders who were celebrated on a national stage.

*\[Press 2: "culture" is struck through; the corrected sentence and three lines appear\]*

But culture will not carry the next four phases. They have to be won through architecture. That means adding structure while keeping the spirit. Making IT a partner, not someone we work around. And turning goodwill into real roles, because a movement cannot run on goodwill forever.

You have built platforms at a scale we can only imagine. Integration, governance, scaling one product across very different markets: those are your problems too. That is the conversation I would love to have with you after this.

*\[Press 3: The close, "Thank you." and two QR codes. Pause for two seconds before the last two lines.\]*

We started before the policy asked us to. Now we will turn that head start into an intelligent hospital, and share it with every hospital in KKM.

Built from within. Shared with all of KKM.

Thank you.

Check these before you present:

- [ ] **135 years versus 1889.** S-01 says the hospital is a hundred and thirty-five years old, but the building drawing is labelled 1889, which makes it 137. Someone may do the sum. Settle on one figure in the deck and the script.
- [ ] **"Eighteen months ago."** April 2025 to now is closer to nineteen months. "Eighteen months" is fine spoken, but don't put it on a slide.
- [ ] **FMS on S-08.** It is read out as on screen. If the audience won't know the abbreviation, say "Family Medicine" instead.
- [ ] **Phase 2 measures.** S-18 skips Phase 2's indicators to save time. If a judge asks, they are: the MyIPO workshop held, key innovations protected through MyIPO, and entries to Karnival Inovasi KKM.
